# Complete Book One — Argument Map

## The governing question

Can a consciousness-first ontology become a disciplined theory that explains
the success of physical and biological description, preserves the irreducible
fact of experience, derives individuation and continuity, and produces a
research program whose outcomes can count against it?

The book does not need to prove that DOT is the final ontology. It must show
that DOT is coherent, non-circular, empirically responsible, comparatively
serious, and capable of revision.

## Core dependency chain

The argument must move in this order:

1. **E0 — Minimal datum:** experience occurs.
2. **EP — Epistemic priority:** evidence usable by an inquirer becomes available
   through experience.
3. **Boundary:** epistemic priority does not entail ontological priority.
4. **H1 — Fundamental-consciousness hypothesis:** consciousness is
   ontologically fundamental.
5. **H2 — Process hypothesis:** fundamental consciousness can differentiate,
   carry state, and preserve consequential continuity.
6. **M1 — Big C model:** H1 and H2 are represented as Big C.
7. **H3 — Individuation hypothesis:** localized centers of experience can arise
   without becoming substances independent of Big C.
8. **M2 — Little c model:** an individuated, perspectival thread of experience.
9. **H4 — Frame hypothesis:** stable constraints permit difference,
   consequence, learning, and intersubjective regularity.
10. **M3 — Reality Frame / Reality Stream:** the rule-bound environment and the
    mediated sequence available to a local experiencer.
11. **H5 — Interface hypothesis:** the organism is the in-Frame interface of
    Little c rather than an exhaustively sufficient producer of experience.
12. **M4 — Canvas / Painting / Character:** persistence, interpretation, and
    expressed pattern within an embodied and social history.
13. **P — Discrimination:** identify observations that should differ if H1–H5
    are true rather than merely redescribing observations accepted by rivals.

No later chapter may be used as retroactive proof of an earlier premise.
Practical usefulness can motivate a model; it cannot establish its ontology.
