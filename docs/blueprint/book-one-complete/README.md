## Relationship to the public edition

The public v3 Digital Edition remains the immutable Reader's Edition. It is the
short, free entry into Digital Organism Theory and must not be rewritten in
place.

This directory develops a separate Complete Book One by expanding the full v3
text in place. V3 supplies the narrative spine and original language. V4 adds
research depth without treating the public book as disposable:

1. state the minimum datum;
2. distinguish that datum from the ontology proposed to explain it;
3. identify every bridge premise;
4. compare the strongest rival explanations;
5. derive downstream architecture in dependency order;
6. specify what could discriminate, revise, or defeat the theory;
7. only then carry the architecture into human and collective life.

Nothing in this directory should be served by the public reader, concept map,
or grounded agent until an editorially frozen manuscript becomes a new,
immutable release. ADR-0018 continues to govern the current public canon.

## The editorial contract

The Complete Book keeps the public edition's moral seriousness and experiential
clarity. It raises the standard of argument.

- Open sections through lived experience when that experience genuinely
  clarifies the problem.
- Move from experience to definition, then from definition to claim.
- Never allow cadence to substitute for a bridge premise.
- Name the claim level before asking the reader to carry its weight.
- Present rival accounts in forms their proponents could recognize.
- Treat neural dependence, perturbation, and correlation as constraints every
  DOT model must explain.
- Mark metaphors as metaphors and provide a mechanism or research obligation
  immediately after them.
- Use equations only when they constrain interpretation, support a derivation,
  or generate a measurable consequence.
- Let a failed prediction count against the relevant DOT claim. Do not protect
  the theory by redescribing every outcome as confirmation.
- Preserve the practical framework even where its truth does not depend on the
  cosmology.

## Claim levels

The public edition distinguishes observation, model, hypothesis, and
speculation. The research manuscript keeps those levels and adds two necessary
links.

| Level | Meaning | Required support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observation** | A first-person datum or publicly measured result | Method, scope, and limits |
| **Definition** | A declared use of a term | Internal consistency and usefulness |
| **Model** | A structured representation of relationships | Explanatory scope and fit |
| **Bridge principle** | A premise carrying the argument between domains | Explicit defense and alternatives |
| **Hypothesis** | A claim about what exists or how it works | Consequences, comparison, and tests |
| **Prediction** | An outcome expected because the hypothesis is true | Operationalization and failure condition |
| **Speculation** | A possibility not yet supported enough to function as a hypothesis | Clear quarantine from derived claims |

Claim labels belong in the claims ledger. They should appear in the prose where
the reader could otherwise mistake a hypothesis for an observation.

## Completion gate

Complete Book One is not complete because every chapter has words. It is
complete when:

- every core term has one stable definition;
- every derivation names its premises and dependency arrows;
- every major claim faces its strongest live alternatives;
- every scientific claim has a traceable primary source;
- every central DOT hypothesis has possible favoring and disfavoring evidence;
- every proposed measurement states what it actually measures;
- metaphor, mechanism, ontology, and empirical observation remain distinct;
- null and negative results have somewhere to go other than reinterpretation;
- the human framework does not use cosmology to excuse suffering or hierarchy;
- the conclusion states what remains useful if the cosmology is wrong.
