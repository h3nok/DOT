# ADR-0026: The Public Entry Teaches the Theory

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-30
- **Deciders:** Founder
- **Amends:** ADR-0022 (single-axis layout and rotating concept sequence)

## Context

ADR-0022 correctly made the front door a note rather than a pitch. It removed
the premature funding and participation asks, kept reading as the primary
action, and required the entry to state claim levels honestly. Later visual
passes, however, made the page's central architecture harder to learn. The hero
separated the ring names from the rings, while the rest of the page moved into
practice and movement language before explaining the ontology the drawing was
meant to introduce.

The founder also clarified a load-bearing relationship: **T and E are outside
Big C**. T names primordial continuity and E the field of possibility. They are
the upstream conditions powering the architecture, not capacities contained by
Big C. The public entry must make that sequence legible without presenting the
diagram's boundaries as measured spatial edges.

The founder further clarified that RF₀ is not presented as the only likely
Reality Frame. DOT treats additional Frames as a strong possibility. The entry
therefore needs to state multiplicity without crowding the hero, drawing other
Frames as spatial shells around this universe, or presenting them as publicly
observed domains.

RF₀ is also not a container located somewhere inside the physical universe.
Within DOT, **RF₀ is exactly the physical universe**—practically encountered and
modeled as a spacetime continuum. Calling RF₀ virtual means that it is proposed
to be generated rather than ontologically fundamental. It does not mean that
physical events are unreal, lawless, or without consequence.

The Big Bang is not described as an event contained within an already existing
RF₀. DOT instead leaves open that it may mark RF₀'s initiating transition: the
beginning of this Frame's physical spacetime history. Even under that reading,
the Big Bang is downstream of T, E, and Big C rather than DOT's ultimate
beginning.

## Decision

The public entry will teach Digital Organism Theory from the outside inward:

1. **T · E** — DOT's foundational postulate: T and E exist as primordial
   continuity and the field of possibility.
2. **Big C** — the developmental hypothesis that a conscious organism emerged
   within T and E, persisted by maintaining coherence, and gave rise to Reality
   Frames and localized experiencers.
3. **RF₀** — the physical universe and practical spacetime continuum, identified
   by DOT as a generated virtual Reality Frame.
4. **Little c** — the proposed local experiencer coupled to an embodied
   interface.
5. **Intent and consequence** — how Little c acts within RF₀ and receives the
   Frame's return.

The hero is the visual centre. Each boundary is named directly on its ring in a
protected gap, and each layer has a distinct theme-derived colour. The figure's
accessible transcription states both the containment order and its epistemic
status.

The hero remains limited to the core RF₀ cross-section and its coupling with
Little c. Its accessible transcription states that RF₀ is the physical universe
and defines virtual as generated rather than unreal. It does not add a second
map for possible Frames. Multiplicity is instead stated in the RF₀ teaching
panel: further Frames are a strong possibility within DOT, may not share RF₀'s
physics, and have not been publicly measured.

The sections immediately below the hero repeat the same four-part order. Each
section provides:

- one plain-language proposition;
- a precise statement of what the model claims;
- an evidence boundary stating what public measurement does not establish; and
- a link into the relevant Book One chapter.

The ten-term vocabulary is an open, finite ledger. Every definition and claim
level is visible at once. It does not rotate, autoplay, or require selection to
read. This replaces ADR-0022's finite rotator: finishing was better than looping,
but simultaneous access is better for comparison and comprehension.

The entry retains ADR-0022's deeper constraints: reading is the primary action;
funding and participation asks remain away from the door; DOT's open problems
remain visible; motion is optional; and the surface owns no literal colour.

The private v4 working manuscript currently places T and E within Big C. That
editorial default now conflicts with the founder-confirmed ontology and must be
reconciled before v4 becomes a release source. This ADR records the decision; it
does not silently rewrite the long-form manuscript.

## Consequences

**Positive.** A reader can identify every boundary in the hero and encounter the
same concepts in the same order below it. The page becomes a finite explanation
of the theory rather than a collection of adjacent themes. Direct labels reduce
legend lookup, the ledger supports comparison, and explicit evidence boundaries
prevent the visual confidence of the diagram from being mistaken for empirical
confirmation. This serves L1, L2, L8, and L10.

Keeping multiplicity in the RF₀ teaching panel prevents the hero from doing two
incompatible jobs. The rings explain one local lawful domain; the prose carries
the wider possibility and its evidence limit.

**Negative.** The page is longer and gives less prominence to practice,
participation, and movement identity. The four theory sections deliberately
repeat some vocabulary from the book. The v4 manuscript requires a foundational
editorial revision before it can become the source of truth for this surface.

**Revisit if** reader testing shows that the four-layer sequence is still being
misread, the ontology changes again during v4 editing, or the landing page and
released Book One no longer make the same claims.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| ------ | ---- | ---- | ------- |
| Keep ring names in a separate legend | More room inside the figure | Requires constant visual lookup; weakens boundary comprehension | Rejected |
| Keep the theory only in the hero | Shorter page | A diagram alone cannot carry definitions and evidence limits | Rejected |
| Lead with practice or participation | More immediately actionable | Changes the subject before the theory is understood | Rejected |
| Draw peer Frames as surrounding shapes | Makes multiplicity immediately pictorial | Implies spatial proximity or nesting that the theory does not establish | Rejected |
| Put RF₀, RF₁, and RF₂ on one arrow | Preserves the timeline grammar | Falsely suggests chronological order or Frame-to-Frame production | Rejected |
| Pair the RF₀ rings with open branches from Big C | Makes non-sequential multiplicity explicit | Crowds the hero and competes with the primary architecture | Rejected |
| State multiplicity in the RF₀ teaching panel | Keeps the hero legible while preserving the claim and evidence boundary | Readers encounter the wider possibility after the hero | **Chosen** |
| Ring labels plus outside-to-inside teaching sections | One visual grammar; explicit claim limits; finite reading path | Longer page and some deliberate repetition | **Chosen** |
