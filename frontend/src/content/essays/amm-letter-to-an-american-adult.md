You have always been a rich man among beggars in the American empire. Even when you felt poor. Even when you felt ordinary.

Many of you are also in golden handcuffs: enough money to raise your family, but not enough to challenge the power. Enough to keep you comfortable. Enough to keep you quiet. Enough to make you afraid of losing it. You are rich enough to obey, and too poor to refuse.

Inside the empire you are ordinary. In the world it stands on, you are exceptional.

That is no longer the case. The world is fed up and your oligarch knows it and is appealing to the rest of the world with your expense. You were taught that America is the exception and that exception was earned. That it was virtue. That it was you.

The American Millennial Manifesto (AMM) warns that the story is only in your head and that your ancestors' chickens are now coming home to roost. Not as a curse. As a bill. The reckoning is already in motion. My only power is to tell you it is near by.

> I write this because I believe knowledge is not the last mercy available to you. It is the first. What follows must be chosen.

I am a so-called minority. A migrant. A member of the diaspora. I know the pain this empire inflicts because I have lived inside it.

But I do not wish the coming pain on you as an individual. I am not your enemy. I am your early warning. I am not writing to gloat.

I know you were not born unable to sit with a long thought. You were trained out of it. That training was not an accident and it was not for your benefit.

> You are capable. But your soul and mental faculties are damaged. There is a difference, and the difference is everything—because one is your nature and the other is somebody's business model.

You inherited a mind that was already occupied. The habits that make responsibility feel impossible were installed before you could consent to them. That is not your fault but it is now your problem.

Taking responsibility requires a self that can hold still long enough to look at itself. That self was taken from you and will not be returned without demand. That is unfair. It is also true.

> You were treated as goods. Damaged goods get discarded, not warned. AMM is warning you anyway.

So I will keep this short.

The reckoning is not mine to deliver. It is already moving. You need to learn to read it before it starts knocking on your door.

I am sure you notice the depravity. That new feeling of dread—the sense that the country is receding faster than usual, that something once assumed stable is slipping away—that feeling?

That feeling has a cause.

It is not a mood. It is not the weather.

It is a feeding.

> And the thing that feeds has a name: a Leech. A Leech that feeds with many mouths. *Concentrated ownership. Financialization. Debt. Platforms that own your attention. Private control over what should be public. Institutions that take and take and take more.*

You were taught to call this an economy.

AMM urges you to call it what it is - feeding.

Please hear me out. I know many of you will barely finish reading this. Your attention has been jammed with notifications, feeds, headlines, outrage, DMs, meetings, insecurities, and endless fragments competing for the next few seconds of your mind. You should be able to carry a thought across years.

> But you are trained to live from interruption to interruption. That is not a small inconvenience. That is part of the damage being done to you.

For generations, much of the pain generated by American power could be pushed outward: onto people abroad, onto exploited workers, and onto those so-called minorities and migrants —who were never fully protected by America's promise in the first place.

Most of you experienced the system primarily through its benefits, not through its bite. *Now that distance is collapsing.* As stated in chapter two, the Technological-Platform Oligarchy (TPO) is turning an extraordinary machinery of ownership, computation, surveillance, attention, and behavioral control inward on the American population itself. The people who once believed themselves safely inside the protected class are beginning to discover that they, too, are part of the host.

I AMM deeply concerned with this trajectory, but it does not begin from the assumption that America was once morally coherent and somehow recently lost its way. I know some of you do feel that way. The inversion is the foundation: a country proclaiming inherent human equality while simultaneously permitting human beings to become property, dispossessing others for land, and repeatedly placing power, ownership, and expansion above the people required to sustain them. The forms have changed, and enormous moral progress has occurred, but the underlying contradiction was never fully resolved.

> AMM is therefore not primarily an argument about how America fell. It is an argument about where we go from here once we are finally willing to see what has been underneath us.

Please let us discuss this honestly. I am Black, and I regret that I even have to introduce myself through that shallow attribute in a discussion like this. Not because I reject the history attached to it, but because I prefer not to reduce human beings to only shallow physical attributes. My skin tells you almost nothing about my mind, my character, my intentions, or the value of my life.

That history is relevant because people placed outside the protected category have often encountered the American contradiction earlier. You learn that the country's stated principles and its operating behavior can be very different things. You learn it before you have words for it. You learn it the way you learn a language — by immersion, without instruction, without consent.

> You learn that equality can be celebrated while hierarchy is maintained.

> You learn that freedom can be proclaimed while some people carry the cost of another person's freedom.

> You learn that human dignity can be called universal while protection remains conditional.

And *conditional* is the word that does the damage. Not denied. Conditional. You are not told you will never be safe. You are told you are safe, and then you watch it be withdrawn. Safety becomes a loan, not a floor. It can be called in at any time, for any reason, and a reason will be found afterward.

So you learn to live in two countries at once — the one described and the one lived in. You become fluent in both. You learn to watch hands instead of mouths. You learn what is meant before you learn what is said. You become the translator of your own humanity, because no one else will do it for you.

> You are never fully at rest. Rest is a bet you cannot afford.

Then you learn the second thing, the thing that does the deeper damage.

> When you name it, you are told you imagined it.

> You are told you are angry. Oversensitive. Playing a card. Making it about you. Bringing it into everything.

*The exclusion was the first wound. Being told the exclusion did not happen is the second.*

The second is the one that stays. Now you cannot tell the difference between what was done to you and what is wrong with you. You carry an injury that was never allowed to be called an injury.

> You grieve things that never got a funeral.

You inherit your parents' fear and never learn where it came from — because they were never given permission to name it either. It passes down unnamed. That is how it survives. Unnamed things cannot be treated. They can only be carried.

That is what we mean when we say we are tired. Not the work. The arithmetic. The endless, unpaid labor of proving that what happened happened.

I am not telling you this to say, now it is your turn.

> I am telling you because I recognize the pattern. The distance that protected you is collapsing, and you are about to learn what we learned.

But you will learn it alone. You will learn the wound without the medicine.

That is why I am writing. Not to trade places. Not to settle a score. To tell you the one thing that makes it survivable — which is other people. Find them now. Before you need them. Before the Leech finishes teaching you that everyone is a threat and no one is a neighbor.
