You may recognize the condition before you recognize the system. You work, pay, comply, and try to keep your family secure. You learn the language expected at work. You manage the bills. You follow the news until following it becomes another source of exhaustion. You still have opinions, preferences, and a vote. Yet decisions that shape your life often arrive already made, and your responsibility is to adjust.

Over time, adjustment can become a way of being. You lower an expectation here, swallow an objection there, postpone something important, and call the compromise maturity. Eventually, you may struggle to distinguish what you chose from what you learned to tolerate. The country continues to describe you as free. Your own life may feel increasingly difficult to direct.

I write because I care about the people living inside this contradiction. I am emerging from this culture too. I have admired its achievements, absorbed its expectations, and tried to build a life within its promises. I know something of the pressure to keep performing while the distance grows between what an institution says and what it asks you to accept.

The American Millennial Manifesto begins with a conviction that comes before any economic argument: **you were born valuable before you produced anything.** Your profession, wealth, credentials, citizenship, and usefulness describe aspects of your circumstances. Your dignity precedes them. A society must be judged by whether its institutions preserve that order or reverse it.

Much of what concerns me is this reversal. A person becomes labor to purchase, attention to capture, data to collect, a balance to finance, a risk to price, or a population to manage. The person remains physically present while the purposes of the institution move further away from the purposes of a human life.

I call the organized reproduction of that reversal **the Leech**.

My argument is that the Leech expresses a logic of domination embedded in Western civilization: the habit of treating power as entitlement, people as instruments, and the ability to impose an arrangement as evidence of its legitimacy. That logic has helped organize conquest, racial hierarchy, and extraction. I argue that it is also eroding the people within contemporary America whose work, trust, and cooperation sustain its power. A civilization becomes dangerous to its own future when accumulating power takes precedence over caring for the life that makes its power possible.

That is the danger I want us to understand. My concern is for the people being consumed, including those who have been taught to admire the arrangement, defend it, and reproduce it against one another.

## What the Leech Means

In this manifesto, **the Leech is a logic of domination made durable through ownership, institutions, and everyday behavior. It converts dependence into extraction, extraction into power, and power into protection from accountability.**

The metaphor begins with a relationship. A leech needs a host. In our social world, accumulated wealth and institutional authority depend on living people: their labor, knowledge, care, cooperation, and willingness to continue. The Leech draws from that life while weakening the public's ability to determine the terms of the relationship.

Its defining feature is the power to keep taking while shifting the human costs elsewhere. An organization can produce useful goods, employ talented people, and perform necessary work while also using its position to impose terms that those who depend on it cannot meaningfully negotiate. Its contribution must be examined alongside its claims on everyone else.

A business earning revenue is not sufficient evidence of this pattern. The relevant questions concern the arrangement: who sets its terms, who can challenge them, who receives the gains, who carries the damage, and what happens when people try to leave. Dependence becomes especially dangerous when the institution controlling a necessity can also obstruct the alternatives.

This gives the metaphor a boundary. A worker's savings, a family's small business, and an institution able to shape markets and public rules occupy different positions. Wealth alone does not explain those differences. Control does. The Leech becomes visible where control over other people's necessities is insulated from reciprocal obligation.

The Leech also comes out of the Host. Its executives, officials, professionals, and customers are formed within a culture. Its institutions recruit from that culture and return incentives, habits, and expectations to it. A society that teaches people to equate accumulation with worth helps produce people willing to organize life around accumulation.

Responsibility remains unequal. Someone complying to keep food on the table does not hold the same power as someone designing the conditions of that compliance. Participation under pressure must be distinguished from authorship, discretion, and control. Recognizing how a system reproduces itself should help us locate responsibility more accurately.

When I write that the Leech “feeds,” “adapts,” or “protects itself,” I am describing recurring institutional behavior. Some harmful conduct is deliberately coordinated. Other conduct emerges through incentives, imitation, competition, and professional self-protection. A claim about a particular plan requires evidence of that plan. The broader pattern can operate through people who disagree about many things while remaining rewarded for the same kind of extraction.

The purpose of the metaphor is to make that pattern easier to inspect. It must remain answerable to what we can establish.

## The Inheritance of Domination

We cannot understand the American condition by beginning with an innocent republic that was recently invaded by corruption. Domination belongs to the country's formation. The original Constitution accommodated slavery through provisions governing apportionment and the return of people escaping bondage. The machinery of representation and the protection of human ownership were written into the same founding document.[^constitution]

That contradiction matters. A society can make consequential commitments to liberty while organizing freedom unequally. Its language of universal dignity can coexist with institutions that distribute dignity according to race, property, and position. The people denied the promise then have to struggle against the arrangements protected in its name.

The wider Western imperial tradition made a related claim: superior power authorized supervision of other peoples. In his July 1885 defense of French colonial expansion, Jules Ferry connected commercial interests and national power with an asserted racial duty to civilize. The parliamentary record also preserves objections to him. Even then, domination was contested; its defenders were making a choice, and others recognized the contradiction.[^ferry]

This is the inheritance I am naming. It is a logic that turns possession into qualification and victory into moral authority. Those who prevail become entitled to explain the world to those over whom they prevail. Resistance can then be described as backwardness, disorder, ingratitude, or a failure to understand what is supposedly being done for one's benefit.

Western societies also contain histories of resistance, scientific achievement, solidarity, and struggles to enlarge freedom. Those histories give us reasons to reject any account that treats domination as a biological destiny or an uncontested cultural essence. The argument concerns institutions and habits that can be identified, defended, changed, or refused.

The civil rights movement belongs within that struggle. Its gains were real. People confronted law, custom, violence, and exclusion to change the conditions of their lives. We diminish their achievement if we describe it as merely a performance absorbed by power. The question that follows is whether institutions continue to surrender unequal power or learn to display the language of equality while preserving their ability to impose unequal terms.

Representation matters. So do ownership, authority, protection, and the ability to refuse abuse. A change in the people occupying senior positions must be examined alongside a change in what those positions allow them to do. An institution can become more representative while keeping its underlying relationship to workers and communities largely intact.

Our task is therefore larger than restoration. We need a standard that does not depend on selecting a comfortable decade and calling it the country's true self. The standard is the living person. Institutions deserve legitimacy to the extent that they protect human dignity, make their power accountable, and permit meaningful correction.

## The Host and the Point of Attachment

**The Host is the United States public: the people whose labor, care, knowledge, trust, and cooperation make the country possible.**

The Host includes the worker, caregiver, patient, student, retiree, tenant, customer, and neighbor. These are overlapping roles in ordinary lives. The same person may supply labor to one institution, debt payments to another, attention to a third, and unpaid care that allows the whole arrangement to continue.

The public also supplies the foundations that private organizations use: infrastructure, education, research, law, and the social trust required for complex cooperation. Land, water, and energy support that activity. Every claim on these foundations affects what remains available for life beyond the claim.

Government is a principal point of attachment because it writes and enforces the terms under which extraction operates. Property rights, procurement, subsidies, taxation, labor protections, competition rules, and policing all shape who can demand what from whom. An arrangement that presents itself as purely private may depend heavily on public decisions and public enforcement.

Government also contains institutions through which the public can challenge domination. Courts, investigations, regulation, public services, and elected bodies can expose or restrain abuses. Their capacity to do so is part of the struggle. The distinction is functional: does an institution protect the public's ability to live and act, or use public authority to secure an insulated claim over it?

Nor does government become innocent whenever private power is involved. Public officials can initiate extraction, cultivate dependence, and protect their own positions. The point of attachment is an active site of decisions, incentives, and responsibility.

In this framework, the Host is the living society. Government is a major instrument through which its relationships are organized. The Leech is the domination that becomes durable through those relationships. Keeping these distinctions clear allows us to examine specific failures without turning “the government,” “the market,” or “the people” into a single undifferentiated actor.

## Two Strategic Heads, One Body

The Leech in the manifesto's imagery has two strategic heads. They represent overlapping forms of organized power. Their shared body is concentrated ownership, finance, and the institutional capacity to preserve claims on the public.

### The Security–Industrial Head

The older head operates through organized force and the material systems that sustain it: war, borders, energy, industry, logistics, and public procurement. It gains leverage where public authorities depend on private suppliers and where the language of security can place decisions beyond ordinary scrutiny.

The danger is the ability of institutions that benefit from a security arrangement to acquire disproportionate influence over its continuation. In 1961, Dwight Eisenhower warned about unwarranted influence arising from the military-industrial complex. His warning included influence that might be sought deliberately or arise without a single directing intention.[^eisenhower]

That distinction remains useful. A contractor may pursue revenue, an official may pursue operational capacity, and a politician may defend jobs in a district. Their interests can converge around continuing a program even when its public value deserves serious challenge. Evaluating it requires access to costs, alternatives, outcomes, and the people who bear its consequences.

The head becomes parasitic when protection of the public becomes subordinate to protection of the arrangement. A threat can be real and still be used to justify spending, secrecy, or coercion beyond what confronting it requires.

### The Technological–Platform Head

The newer head operates through computation, platforms, data, cloud infrastructure, artificial intelligence, and control over access. Its power grows when participation in work, communication, commerce, or public life requires passage through privately governed systems.

A platform can provide a useful service while acquiring authority over those who depend on it. It can set the terms of visibility, access, pricing, and participation. As dependence deepens, leaving may require abandoning customers, professional relationships, accumulated work, or infrastructure that cannot easily be moved.

The FTC's January 2025 examination of partnerships linking Microsoft and OpenAI, Amazon and Anthropic, and Alphabet and Anthropic identified contractual rights, cloud spending commitments, and potential switching costs. Those findings raised concerns about competition and dependence.[^ai-partnerships] The institutional question is clear: who controls the infrastructure through which others must develop and deliver their work?

Computation has owners. AI systems require chips, servers, networks, electricity, cooling, and people able to maintain them. As institutions route more decisions through these systems, ownership and the ability to inspect or challenge their use become public questions. Technical capability does not settle who should hold that authority.

### The Shared Body

The two heads already overlap. In December 2022, the Defense Department selected Amazon Web Services, Google, Microsoft, and Oracle for its Joint Warfighting Cloud Capability. That procurement is a concrete connection between commercial computing and military infrastructure.[^jwcc]

The connection alone does not establish abuse. It identifies a relationship that deserves scrutiny: public authority, commercial dependence, technical expertise, and security power operating together. Their integration can make accountability more difficult when responsibility is divided across agencies, contractors, and systems the public cannot inspect.

The shared body is what allows the benefits to accumulate: ownership claims, financial arrangements, contractual control, and influence over the conditions of competition. Particular firms and factions can compete fiercely while benefiting from a common rule that gives owners more authority over an essential system than the people dependent on it.

## Three Layers of the Same Relationship

The Leech operates through three connected layers: material, political, and epistemic. The third concerns how people come to know, interpret, and judge their world.

The material layer takes from what sustains a life. It organizes claims on labor, time, money, land, energy, attention, and data. Its power becomes visible in the cost of housing, the terms of employment, the price of necessary care, and the difficulty of finding alternatives when those terms become harmful.

The political layer secures the arrangement. It concerns who can influence the rules, obtain public support, shape enforcement, and make the costs of challenge fall on someone else. A profitable practice becomes more durable when those benefiting from it can influence the institutions responsible for correcting it.

The epistemic layer teaches the public how to understand the first two. It supplies explanations for why the arrangement is natural, why its beneficiaries deserve their position, and why objections reveal weakness, ignorance, or disloyalty. It can convert an institutional problem into a defect in the person experiencing it.

This layer is especially powerful when the Host begins doing its work without being instructed. A worker dismisses another worker's concern as entitlement. A parent teaches a child to admire wealth without asking how it was acquired. A citizen treats the humiliation of an unfamiliar group as proof that order is being restored. People enforce the standards against one another because those standards have become part of their own judgment.

The layers reinforce one another. Dependence makes refusal costly. Political protection makes the arrangement difficult to change. Cultural approval makes refusal appear unreasonable. A person can still make choices within these conditions while having little influence over the conditions themselves.

That is what I mean by training obedience. It can happen through repeated lessons about which questions cost you approval, which observations threaten your career, and which forms of suffering you are expected to absorb quietly. The lesson becomes durable when you begin censoring yourself before anyone needs to ask.

## Inversion

The Leech's signature method is **inversion**: an institution claims moral authority from the purpose its conduct is undermining.

Protection can become a reason to expand unaccountable power. Efficiency can become a reason to transfer work and risk onto people with less capacity to carry them. Opportunity can become a promise used to sell a dependency. The language remains reassuring while the terms of the relationship move in the opposite direction.

These words have legitimate meanings. We test their use by following the consequences. If a service claims to empower you, examine whether your ability to understand, choose, leave, and seek correction actually improves. If a reform promises accountability, identify who can impose a consequence and whether the people harmed can reach that process.

The gap between promise and practice is a starting point for inquiry. It does not, by itself, prove a hidden plan. The next step is to examine decisions, records, incentives, and responses to evidence of harm. What happens after the damage becomes known often tells us more than the institution's original promise.

A concrete case is Purdue Pharma's 2020 guilty plea to federal fraud and kickback conspiracies. The company admitted conduct that included misleading the Drug Enforcement Administration and paying to encourage prescriptions of its opioid products. It also admitted continuing to market to providers it had good reason to believe were diverting opioids.[^purdue]

The significance is specific and severe: commercial incentives corrupted practices situated within the provision of care. Human vulnerability supplied a market, and the conduct admitted in court helped expand sales. We can identify the institution, the conduct, and the mechanism without making every medical professional an accomplice.

That is the standard this framework should meet. Name the behavior. Establish the relationship. Trace the benefit. Identify the cost. Then ask what allowed the arrangement to continue.

## The Leech and Its Offspring

Domination needs ordinary institutions to become ordinary life. It needs people to administer, explain, normalize, and enforce arrangements they may never examine as a whole.

I call these recurring functions the Leech's **offspring**. Four are especially important to this chapter: Smile, Mask, Suit, and Shield. They operate through entertainment, media, politics, and security. An institution may perform several functions at once. A professional may resist them within the same organization where another professional advances them.

The distinction concerns what the work does to the public's agency. It gives us a way to examine conduct without assuming that everyone in an occupation shares the same purpose.

### Offspring Smile: The Manufacture of Appetite

Smile works through pleasure, aspiration, familiarity, and belonging. It makes an arrangement attractive enough that people pursue it before examining what it asks of them.

Entertainment is part of a good human life. I have enjoyed this culture's movies, sports, and shared experiences. The concern begins when our need for pleasure and relief becomes a reliable means of capturing attention, selling insecurity, and teaching us what to admire.

The child encounters a world in which wealth receives endless visibility. The adult meets an uninterrupted stream of lives against which to measure an ordinary home, body, relationship, or career. There is always another improvement to purchase, another status to pursue, another reason to feel behind. Under that arrangement, dissatisfaction has commercial value.

The digital version adds continuous measurement. The FTC's September 2024 study of major social media and video streaming services described extensive collection and monetization of personal information, incentives to keep users engaged, and inadequate protections for children and teens. It provides a concrete basis for examining the business relationship beneath the experience on the screen.[^social-media]

My concern is what happens when the commercial measure of a successful encounter is how long you remain available to the system. Your need for rest, your emotional vulnerability, and your child's desire to belong enter a process organized around goals that may have little to do with your well-being.

Smile is strongest when the public begins confusing visibility with value. We learn whom to admire by how often they appear. We learn what a successful life looks like from people whose income may depend on keeping us dissatisfied with our own.

The loss is larger than wasted time. A person needs some unclaimed attention in which to notice, reflect, and choose. A community needs shared experiences whose purpose is participation rather than the sale of access to its members. Those are conditions of freedom worth defending.

### Offspring Mask: The Management of Interpretation

Mask works on what an event is allowed to mean. It directs attention, distributes credibility, and supplies the emotional context in which the public receives information.

A report can contain accurate facts while leaving decisive relationships unexplained. A victim can appear as a person with a name, a family, and a future. Another can appear as a number, a security problem, or an unfortunate complication. The difference directs the reader's moral attention before any explicit instruction is given.

For this reason, the gap between what is published and what is understood deserves examination. Look at who receives sustained attention, whose explanation is treated as authoritative, which uncertainties are admitted, and which questions repeatedly disappear. Also look at the records behind the presentation: the contract, the budget, the court finding, the original statement.

Professional confidence can make this inquiry difficult. A polished presentation asks to be received as settled knowledge. Expertise deserves attention within its field, but expertise also requires evidence, limits, and the possibility of correction. Institutional rank does not remove those obligations.

The same discipline applies to the person promising to expose every official lie. Distrust is also an audience, and outrage is also a market. Someone can profit from your alienation while giving you another story that makes independent judgment unnecessary. The test is whether the work helps you investigate reality or demands loyalty to its interpreter.

I want this manifesto held to that standard. A metaphor should sharpen your questions. If it becomes an explanation that cannot be challenged by evidence, it will reproduce the intellectual submission it claims to oppose.

The public needs journalism, investigation, disagreement, and institutions that can earn trust through correction. Mask operates where trust is demanded as an inheritance while the practices that justify it are neglected.

### Offspring Suit: The Containment of Political Choice

Suit translates organized interests into policy, public legitimacy, and the boundaries of acceptable debate. Its language is representation. Its danger is the conversion of representation into a process that keeps the public engaged while leaving decisive concentrations of power insulated.

Political rights matter. Elections affect law, appointments, public services, and the possibilities of reform. Those consequences are reasons to use political rights seriously. They are also reasons to examine who can sustain influence between elections, when rules are drafted, contracts negotiated, enforcement priorities chosen, and appointments made.

Consider a visible feature of the system. The Federal Election Commission explains that Super PACs may receive unlimited contributions from individuals, corporations, unions, and other PACs to finance independent political activity. Independent expenditures must meet requirements separating them from candidate coordination.[^campaign-finance] The formal distinction is important. So is the unequal capacity to purchase repeated access to public attention.

The concern is a durable difference in political capacity. An exhausted citizen has a vote and limited time. A well-funded organization can employ specialists, monitor legislation, maintain relationships, commission arguments, and intervene repeatedly. Whether its position serves the public must be examined on the merits; its resources should never be mistaken for the public's consent.

Policy toward Israel belongs within this scrutiny. AIPAC states its aim of persuading the United States government to enact policies supporting the U.S.–Israel relationship. Its proposals and political activity should be evaluated through the same examination of interests, influence, and consequences applied to other lobbying organizations.[^aipac] The Congressional Research Service documents American military assistance and arms-related support to Israel, including additional assistance following October 7, 2023.[^israel-aid]

These are public relationships involving decisions, resources, and responsibility. American participation must be examined alongside the choices of Israeli authorities and the consequences for Palestinians. Jewish people are not interchangeable with the Israeli government or any lobbying organization. Historical persecution gives us an obligation to oppose persecution; it supplies no exemption from scrutiny for a state. Palestinian and Israeli civilian lives carry the same intrinsic worth.

Suit succeeds when people defend a political identity more carefully than they examine the conduct performed under it. Party loyalty can then become a reason to excuse what would be intolerable from an opponent. The public's responsibility is to keep its standards attached to conduct, especially when applying them becomes inconvenient.

### Offspring Shield: The Enforcement of the Arrangement

Shield operates through force, surveillance, secrecy, classification, and the threat of consequences. It concerns the institutions empowered to investigate, compel, detain, and use violence. Their authority makes public accountability indispensable.

There is documented history behind this concern. The Senate's Church Committee investigated abuses by American intelligence agencies and examined the FBI's COINTELPRO operations against political groups and individuals, including civil rights and antiwar targets. Its findings connected abuses to failures of checks and accountability. Its work also helped produce institutional reforms.[^church]

That record establishes both a danger and a possibility. Institutions charged with protecting a country have acted against people exercising their rights. Other public institutions have exposed those actions and attempted to restrain them. The outcome depends on authority being contestable, records being recoverable, and people being willing to act on what they find.

Shield becomes an instrument of domination when protecting a powerful arrangement takes precedence over protecting the people subject to it. Secrecy obstructs correction. Selective enforcement teaches people that standing matters more than conduct. Retaliation makes a formal right difficult to exercise. The public begins adjusting to the prospect of punishment even where the rules still promise protection.

Locate responsibility in the exercise of power: who authorized an action, what evidence justified it, who reviewed it, and whether the people affected have a meaningful remedy. Examine what officials do when abuse becomes known. The response can expose protection of wrongdoing or reveal people willing to confront it.

The four offspring reinforce one another. Smile makes a way of life desirable. Mask supplies its interpretation. Suit secures its political terms. Shield can enforce them. Their cooperation may be explicit, routine, or indirect. Our work is to establish which relationships actually exist and what they do.

## Racism and the Public's Participation

Racism belongs inside this analysis because domination requires a way of distributing human worth. Racial hierarchy makes unequal treatment appear deserved, natural, or necessary. It tells people whose pain deserves an explanation and whose pain requires only an excuse.

In 2015, the Justice Department's investigation of Ferguson found that the police department and municipal court operated under an excessive focus on revenue generation, alongside racial bias and violations of constitutional rights. The investigation connected those practices to discrimination against African Americans and damage to public trust.[^ferguson]

The relationship is concrete. An institution with coercive authority treated the public as a source of revenue, and racial inequality shaped the burden. This example also makes clear why the Leech cannot be reduced to private corporations alone. Public institutions can reproduce the same extractive logic through their own incentives and decisions.

Racism also lives in ordinary judgments. It appears in assumptions about intelligence, danger, competence, belonging, and whose account of an experience deserves belief. Repetition can make these assumptions feel like observation. The person carrying them may experience an inherited ranking as simple common sense.

People harmed by hierarchy can absorb it too. An exploited worker can defend the degradation of another worker. Someone excluded from one circle can police the boundary of a smaller circle in which they have standing. A person subjected to racism can reproduce racist judgments against others or turn those judgments inward. Suffering does not automatically teach us how domination works.

That is a painful part of the Host's condition. A person may be seeking relief while reaching for the same ranking that helped produce the injury. The promise is that someone else will remain beneath them, and that relative position will feel like security.

The alternative requires a different standard: dignity that does not depend on finding a lower-ranked person. It requires us to examine the satisfaction we receive from another person's humiliation, including when our preferred institutions approve of it.

This responsibility extends beyond race to class, nationality, religion, gender, and professional standing. The histories and mechanisms differ. The common moral failure is making basic human regard conditional on position. A movement against domination must be willing to correct that failure within itself.

## The Public Learns to Police Itself

A culture reaches deeply into a person before that person has the language to examine it. We inherit accounts of success, authority, normality, and danger. We learn which accents sound educated, which careers deserve respect, which countries are entitled to judge others, and which kinds of wealth require no explanation.

Some of what we inherit is useful. Some of it is contradictory. Much of it arrives before we can compare it with anything else. Education adds tools, but a credential does not certify that the frame through which we use those tools has been examined.

I call it indoctrination when an inherited explanation is protected from examination and obedience is rewarded as understanding. An ideology becomes a closed world when it supplies an answer in advance of every encounter. The person then meets evidence through a story already deciding what the evidence is allowed to mean. Contradiction becomes an inconvenience to explain away.

This helps explain the confusion I am concerned about. A person can experience insecurity while remaining attached to a picture of society that makes the insecurity unintelligible. If the country is assumed to reward everyone according to merit, persistent difficulty must mean personal failure or someone else's undeserved advantage. The institutions setting the terms escape the question. The person turns against themselves or against another person struggling beside them.

This is why intelligence alone offers insufficient protection. A technically capable person can solve difficult problems inside an arrangement while accepting its purpose as given. An engineer improves a system's performance. A manager makes compliance easier to measure. A lawyer makes an institution's position more defensible. Each may perform excellent work while leaving the human purpose of the whole untouched.

I write as an engineer and an AI scientist. I know the appeal of the solvable technical problem. But a system's ability to execute its purpose says nothing, by itself, about whether that purpose deserves to govern other people's lives. Better performance can make a harmful arrangement more effective.

The professional's dependence complicates this recognition. Salary, insurance, retirement, equity, and family obligations give institutional approval immediate weight. These are the golden handcuffs: benefits and responsibilities that make an objection feel like a threat to the life you have built.

There is no dignity in mocking someone for needing income or protecting a family. The important distinction is between understanding a compromise and elevating it into a moral doctrine. You may need to remain in a job. You do not have to declare every practice of the organization wise, every senior person deserving, or every injured colleague unreasonable.

The public begins policing itself when these declarations become the price of belonging. People learn to make the correct face in the meeting, to laugh at the appropriate target, to treat an uncomfortable fact as a failure of attitude. Eventually the institution can rely on colleagues to discipline one another.

Admiration performs similar work. If the ultimate image of success is the person with enough power to escape ordinary obligations, the aspiration is to reach that position. The arrangement reproduces itself through people waiting for their turn to benefit from it.

**In Power We Trust** becomes the practice, whatever the declared creed. We ask who has prevailed before asking what they have done. We teach deference upward and suspicion downward, then wonder why public life feels so difficult to repair.

An emancipated public needs a different ambition. It must be possible to want a secure, capable, meaningful life without wanting authority to diminish someone else's. Our children should be able to admire competence, care, courage, and honest contribution without learning that accumulation is proof of superior human worth.

## A Civilization Consuming Its Host

The Leech depends on the Host, but that dependence does not guarantee restraint. The rewards for taking more can arrive sooner than the consequences of taking too much. The gains can be concentrated while the damage is dispersed across people who lack the power to make it count.

A firm can improve its own position while leaving workers less secure. An owner can raise the return on an asset while making a community harder to live in. An institution can reduce its costs by transferring responsibilities to families already carrying more than they can manage. Each decision may look successful from inside the organization. Together, such decisions can undermine the conditions on which the society depends.

**Suffering can be profitable.** A person's urgent need, inability to leave, or lack of alternatives can strengthen another party's bargaining position. A market can reward the continued management of a dependency even when ending that dependency would be better for the person living with it. The fact that money changes hands does not settle whether the relationship serves human life.

This is what I mean by a civilization consuming its own Host. It draws down the people's time, patience, health, trust, and capacity to care, then treats the resulting difficulty as another problem for individuals to solve. The institution's performance can look healthy while the people supporting it become exhausted.

Much of the damage is experienced privately. There is the parent who reaches the end of the day with too little patience left for a child. The worker who stops offering honest judgment because judgment has become professionally expensive. The household that postpones a necessary decision because one disruption would destabilize everything. The person who feels ashamed of struggling inside a system that continually advertises other people's success.

These are experiences to examine, not a diagnosis to impose on every American. They also cannot be explained by one cause. But when many institutions repeatedly demand more adaptation from people while surrendering little power themselves, it becomes reasonable to investigate the arrangement rather than locating every failure inside the individual.

Erosion is especially difficult to confront because the person is still functioning. The shift is completed. The bill is paid. The meeting is attended. From a distance, the life appears intact. What has diminished may be the person's ability to imagine, refuse, connect, or recover.

The moral cost deserves recognition before the person becomes visibly incapacitated. A society should not require collapse as proof that its terms are injuring people.

## The Children Are the Test

There is a question that makes institutional language difficult to hide behind: **what happens when protecting a child threatens a powerful adult or a respected organization?**

Every institution can announce that children matter. The test begins when acting on that statement threatens reputation, income, access, or a career. It begins with whether a child's account receives serious attention, whether evidence is preserved, whether risk is addressed, and whether the process protects the person reporting harm.

The Justice Department inspector general's 2021 investigation of the FBI's handling of allegations against Larry Nassar documented fundamental failures, policy violations, and inadequate action to address an ongoing threat. It also found that Indianapolis officials supplied incomplete or inaccurate information when their handling of the matter came under scrutiny.[^nassar]

The 2020 Office of Professional Responsibility review of the earlier federal Jeffrey Epstein investigation reached a different set of findings. It did not find professional misconduct by Department attorneys, but it found that victims had not received the forthright and sensitive treatment expected of the Department and criticized Alexander Acosta's judgment concerning the agreement and victim notification.[^epstein]

These findings must be stated accurately. The differences between them matter. So does the common moral question: how did institutions with duties of protection respond when vulnerable people needed those duties fulfilled?

The damage extends beyond a single failure to act. When victims encounter delay, disbelief, or institutional self-protection, the burden of making the institution behave responsibly falls back onto people already harmed. Others watching may learn that telling the truth brings exposure while authority retains the benefit of ambiguity.

Children must never become props in a political performance. Their safety requires patient, competent work: prevention, access to trusted adults, reliable reporting, serious investigation, and support that continues after public attention moves elsewhere. Accountability also requires fair procedures capable of establishing what happened and who was responsible.

This is a test of what our institutions are for. A society that spends immense energy celebrating power should be judged by the protection it makes available to someone with almost none. Human worth must be most secure where the person has the least capacity to bargain for it.

## The Danger Ahead

I believe further oppression is a serious danger if these relationships deepen. Dependence gives institutions leverage. Surveillance gives them information. Concentrated control over infrastructure can make exclusion more costly. Weak accountability makes the misuse of those capacities harder to correct.

The warning concerns what becomes possible when these capabilities converge. A person may face restrictions whose reasons they cannot inspect, decisions they cannot meaningfully appeal, or costs of dissent they cannot afford. A public accustomed to accepting such conditions in one part of life may become less prepared to resist their expansion elsewhere.

That is a risk to investigate and prevent. Examine what a power permits, what prevents its misuse, who can challenge it, and whether those safeguards work for people without wealth or connections. A right that requires exceptional resources to exercise offers little protection to the person who cannot obtain them.

For many people, coercion is already part of the present. Racism, abuse of authority, and institutional retaliation do not begin when previously protected groups notice their own insecurity. Their experiences should help us recognize the mechanism earlier. A society that accepts diminished rights for an unfamiliar group weakens its own ability to defend rights as a principle.

The public's confusion matters here. People can sense that life is becoming harder while interpreting the cause through the very rankings that keep them divided. Anger then travels toward a migrant, a neighbor, a racial group, or another worker. The institutions setting the terms receive less scrutiny than the people struggling inside them.

I am warning against that misdirection. A person experiencing genuine injury deserves an account that helps locate its causes. Supplying a vulnerable target may create emotional relief while leaving the relationship producing the injury untouched.

There is no responsible timetable for declaring the country's fate inevitable. There is a responsibility to recognize dangerous arrangements while they remain contestable. Fear should direct our attention toward that work, rather than becoming another instrument through which someone claims authority over us.

## Learn to Trace the Relationship

Recognizing the Leech should produce a method of inquiry. Start with a specific condition in your life or community. Identify the decision, institution, contract, rule, or ownership claim shaping it. Move from a general feeling of powerlessness toward a relationship that can be examined.

Follow the money: who receives payment, returns, subsidies, or protection from loss? Then follow the power to compel: who can enforce the arrangement, remove access, impose a penalty, or make leaving difficult? Follow legitimacy: who presents the arrangement as necessary, and what evidence supports that claim? Finally, examine the questions people are discouraged from asking, along with the actual consequences of asking them.

Keep distinctions intact. Revenue is different from profit. A shareholder's claim is different from a manager's discretion. Allegations differ from findings. An institution's stated purpose differs from its demonstrated conduct. A useful analysis identifies where these things meet and where they do not.

Look for what would change your conclusion. If an institution corrects a harm, permits meaningful challenge, or gives up an unfair advantage, that matters. If a preferred commentator distorts a record, that matters too. Loyalty to a diagnosis should never become stronger than the obligation to understand what is happening.

The same scrutiny belongs inside the household, workplace, and movement. Notice whose judgment you dismiss because of rank. Notice when you reward obedience while claiming to value thought. Notice whether you defend someone's dignity only after learning that they belong to your side.

This is how the analysis becomes personal without becoming self-blame. We inherit conditions we did not design. We also exercise some discretion within them. Responsibility begins with using that discretion more honestly and building the capacity to change what an isolated person cannot.

## A Bootstrapping Revolution

A public cannot think its way out of dependence while leaving all the conditions of dependence untouched. Clarity has to become organized capacity: the ability to protect one another, preserve knowledge, demand correction, and sustain alternatives.

That is what I mean by a bootstrapping revolution. People begin with the skills, relationships, institutions, and resources available to them. They use those resources to enlarge their ability to act together. The work proceeds through practical gains that make the next gain possible.

Some of this work belongs inside existing institutions. Organize workers. Defend honest professional judgment. Support investigative reporting. Make public procurement and decision-making inspectable. Protect people who disclose wrongdoing. Demand that necessary services be governed by obligations to the people who depend on them.

Some of it requires building alternatives. The manifesto calls these **Parallel Systems**: institutions through which communities gain a meaningful share of ownership, knowledge, decision-making, and correction. A cooperative, a community land arrangement, a local information service, or shared technical infrastructure earns that description through its actual governance and usefulness.

The name confers nothing. A community project can reproduce secrecy, hierarchy, and self-protection. A new platform can become another toll collector. People building an alternative must make its finances understandable, its authority limited, its decisions challengeable, and its responsibilities durable beyond the goodwill of a founder.

The aim is to make refusal materially possible. A worker with collective support can face a different set of choices from a worker alone. A community with an alternative source of an essential service has more room to negotiate. People who can retain and move their own knowledge are less dependent on the institution currently holding it.

These efforts also require public action at a larger scale. Communities cannot individually repair every market, enforce every right, or substitute for every public service. Building local capacity should strengthen the demand for accountable public institutions and wider cooperation. The scale of the response must be sufficient for the power being challenged.

This work requires a moral discipline as well as an organizational one. We cannot build freedom while cultivating contempt for the people still caught in the arrangement. Humiliation deepens the need to defend an identity. Care makes a different conversation possible, one in which a person can revise a belief without surrendering their dignity.

Love has a practical meaning here: the life of another person remains real to you when their suffering becomes inconvenient to acknowledge. It means refusing to make their disposability the condition of your security. It also means holding people accountable for the power they exercise, including people you admire.

The Leech depends on a public that supplies its resources, accepts its rankings, and struggles to act together. Every durable increase in the public's capacity to understand, care, refuse, and govern its own institutions changes that relationship.

I write because I want the people living inside this civilization to have a future worth inhabiting. Its achievements give us capacities we can use. Its history gives us obligations we must face. Its failures give us work to do.

The purpose of that work is the person: the child whose life is beginning, the adult trying to remain whole, the elder whose worth survives the end of employment, the neighbor whose suffering has been kept outside our concern.

**Human life is the reason for the institution. Our task is to make that order real.**
